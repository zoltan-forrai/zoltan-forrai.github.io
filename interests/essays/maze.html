Christopher Manson's Maze

What Is Maze?

Maze is an immersion puzzle book1. from 1985 written and illustrated by Christo­pher Manson.

In Maze you navigate a building of 45 deceptive rooms with the goal of reaching the 45th room in the centre of the maze. Each room you enter has a set of numbered doors leading to other rooms. Clues are hidden within the illustration and narrative as to which is the "correct" door to pick. Once you've figured out what the room is telling you, or you picked a door at random, you turn to the page denoted by the number. And thus, you navigate the maze, lead, or mislead by its cryptic clues. Will you find room 45? Or will you be trapped forever?

The Riddle of the Maze

Getting to the 45th room will be difficult enough on its own, and yet the real puzzle of the book only starts from there. There is a riddle in room 45, in the form of a question. Before you can attempt to answer it however, you must first figure out what the question is. It's hidden somewhere in room 45.

Once you have it, look for the answer. There will be many paths to room 45, but only one, the shortest of 16 steps, has the answer to the riddle.

Solve the World's Most Challenging Puzzle

How hard is Maze? When the book was first printed the publisher ran a competition. The first to solve the riddle would receive $10,000.

Three rounds of clues went out, but no one could answer the riddle. The solution was later revealed by the publisher.

Maybe you can solve it without looking at the clues? I believe. :)

Does Maze Have a Story?

Not in the traditional sense. There is a narrative, a setting, characters who engage in dialogue but there is no formal plot to take you through the book. There are some continuity between adjacent rooms (a ringing is heard in one, a telephone is seen in the other) but don't go in expecting a CYOA.

Characters remain unnamed; even the identity of the guide (the narrator) is only ever hinted at. The focus of the book, and a character in itself is the setting: the maze and its many rooms.
