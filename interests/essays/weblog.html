Welcome to My Weblog?

The internet is dead, and it's a gruesome crime scene. The coroner's report is clear; the web was killed.

The online creator sphere is in a fragile state right now. The once-working contract between platform and creator is breaking. It was once quite simple: users only visited sites if there was content to be seen, therefore platforms needed creators to make content to pull those users in, and in return the creator was rewarded with some of the revenue generated. The creator provided the content, the platform provided the infrastructure. It was a deal many built careers on online.

This deal is no longer desirable for platforms. Creativity is unreliable, it's messy, it's altogether too human. And when your singular goal is profit, humanity becomes a liability. Just imagine how much revenue they could save, if only they didn't need creatives to make content. Well, enter Ai...

I'm not on social media anymore; the malefits plain outweigh the benefits.1. They are all too eager to replace human creativity with artificial crap. But all Ai has done so far is pollute the web, turning the internet into a reeking landfill. No matter how I look at it, to me, social media looks like a sinking ship.

The only platform, I believe, that escapes this criticism is Newgrounds. With 30 years of history, Newgrounds stands as an exemplary case against web corporatisation: an artist-focused architecture, no evil algorithm, a strong and committed community, and a firm stance against GenAi. While I don't wish NG to become mainstream (that's the worst thing that can happen to a niche), I do see a future where Newgrounds becomes a bigger player on the web.

Time moves like a pendulum; it swings, forth and back between extremes, never quite reaching equilibrium.2. If that is so, then the present's push towards the artificial will be mirrored by an equal swing back to being human. Maybe wishful thinking from my part, but I'm hopeful for what is to come.

So Why Make a Website Now?

A festering corpse makes for fertile soil and flourishing crops. In other words, I'm betting on a better future. Now, I don't have delusions; I know all too well we are like frogs, perfectly content in our boiling pond. And yet, like frogs, we can jump, if we find the hot tub getting too toasty.

I jumped. What's stopping you? Is there something you gain from the corporate web that makes the trade worth it for you?

There are alternatives, you know. There's a resurgence in the creation of handcrafted personal websites.3. Turns out, coding a website is rather fun. Who would have thunk? For me, it's exactly the right mixture of technical and creative that I enjoy. Learning HTML and CSS is quite easy too and only takes a few weeks to a month; and in most cases you don't need JavaScript at all.

I do not think the IndieWeb is the panacea to all the internet's ills. I only see it as a single good step in the right direction, on a path I prefer.

Building this website has become a gratifying hobby of mine. Keep in mind, however, that I'm no developer, just an English teacher who likes computers, so there are most definitely bugs on my site.4.

What Is This Website For?

Simply, I wish to share what I create and do it on my own terms. Not one of the existing writing platforms aligns with my values (see above), and I'm not yet good enough to publish in magazines. That leaves this, and this, I suppose, would be a form of self-publishing.

"Aren't you afraid that Ai can scrape your site and train on your writing?"
No, it does not bother me at all. It probably already has.

Who Is This Website For?

First and foremost, I write for myself. But if you've got this far, you might enjoy my other works as well. This is my latest piece.

Everything I write on here is free to read. The only thing I ask in return is your feedback. So if you have anything on your mind, share it with me, I would greatly appreciate it. Feel free to message me for any reason at all. As a writer, I don't get to socialise much.

Should I even Write?

I'm not a native English speaker. I don't even have a degree in literature, let alone one in creative writing (I have a half-finished master's in TESL).

But I do enjoy language and literature, and maybe I can write something SO idiosyncratic that it becomes entertaining to read.

So no, probably not.
